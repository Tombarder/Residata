The data comes from the publicly published price lists of Slovak developers, which Residata reads daily, flat by flat. Supply consists of available and reserved flats in active projects; homes only — no parking, storage or commercial units. Prices and floor areas are means over the flats whose developer publishes a price, which is 72.5% of the offer. Price levels are computed as the mean of daily values over the last two weeks of the quarter, so that no single day on which a project added or withdrew part of its offer can move them. Months to clear is current supply divided by the monthly pace of sales in that quarter; it is shown only for towns with at least 10 sales, because below that it is one project's decision rather than a town's pace.

1 October 2026 | QUARTERLY REVIEW OF THE SLOVAK NEW-BUILD MARKET

# Slovakia: one town clears its offer in 12.6 months, another in 53.5

At the close of the quarter the supply of new-build flats in Slovakia stood at 9 380 across 45 towns. Average prices and the size of the offer differ between them, but the largest difference is not in price — it is in how fast that offer sells.

Bratislava accounts for 43.1% of the whole offer and 51.9% of sales; the other 44 towns share 5 338 flats between them. On price the range is a factor of 2.5: Bratislava at 5 722 €/m² incl. VAT against Lučenec at 2 320.

Speed, however, does not follow price. At the current pace the offer in Trenčín would clear in 12.6 months and in Banská Bystrica in 53.5 — a factor of 4.2. Trenčín averages 4 005 €/m² incl. VAT and Banská Bystrica 3 884, which is lower. Across the 11 towns where the pace can be computed, the relationship between price and clearing speed is effectively nil (a correlation of 0.07). What a town costs tells you nothing about how fast it sells.

The difference between towns is easier to see in floor area than in percentages. For the price of an average Bratislava flat — 382 814 € incl. VAT — you get 67 m² in Bratislava. In Lučenec it is 165 m², in Lužianky 156 m² and in Spišská Nová Ves 150 m².

## A view on what the table says

Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata: "How fast an offer clears is more useful to a developer and to a buyer than the price itself. It says how much time the market has to absorb what is on sale — and whether there is room to add more.

The table makes it clear that price and speed are not related as directly as one would expect. Towns at similar price levels clear at different speeds, and the slowest towns are not the dearest.

For a buyer it means the pressure to decide quickly is different in every town. Where the offer would clear within a year it is worth acting; where it would take four, there is time to choose."

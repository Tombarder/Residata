MARKET ANALYSIS · The new-build map · 21 September 2026

# In two thirds of Slovak towns the whole new-build supply is at most two projects. The town's average price is their price list

Slovak towns outside Bratislava have spent a decade building more than ever before. Almost nothing is published about how they sell: the regular new-build analysis covers Bratislava, and beyond it prices appear as regional and city averages. An average quietly assumes that there is a market underneath it to average. In most Slovak towns what lies underneath is one or two price lists.

**In this issue:** how many projects make up the supply in each of the 43 Slovak towns where a new-build flat can be bought today · what happens to a town's price when one project is taken out of it · how far a price moves outside Bratislava.

---

New-build flats are sold today in every Slovak regional capital and in dozens of smaller towns. A new development in Prešov, Zvolen or Liptovský Mikuláš is an ordinary offer, and the prices they sell at appear regularly in regional statistics.

What those prices mean is nowhere examined. The regular new-build analyses cover Bratislava, where there is enough to count; outside it, averages are published by region or by regional capital and nobody shows what they are made of. The difference between what such an average measures in Bratislava and what it measures in Trenčín is not academic.

So the question is not how high a regional average is. It is how many price lists it is made of, and what happens to it when one of them moves. The answer decides whether a developer can lean on it when pricing a phase, and whether a buyer can read from it what counts as expensive in their town.

The answer differs in every town and can be quantified. In Bratislava the largest project holds 11 % of the town's supply. In Banská Bystrica, 60 %. And in 18 of 43 towns the largest project holds all of it, because it is the only one.

Those shares come from very differently sized offers. Of the 7 349 available flats that make up the entire Slovak new-build supply, 49 % stand in a single city: 3 633 flats in Bratislava, across 96 projects, not one of which exceeds 11 % of the supply. The remaining 3 716 flats are spread across 42 towns, and 27 of those have at most two projects on offer. The median unit price is 5 314 €/m² incl. VAT in Bratislava and 3 676 €/m² incl. VAT in the rest of the country.

![Largest project's share of a town's supply](/analyzy/mapa-novostavieb-2026-09-1-jeden-projekt-en.svg)
*Largest project's share of available flats, towns with at least 90 available.*

What this does to a price is clearest in Banská Bystrica. At a median of 3 928 €/m² incl. VAT it is the 4th dearest of the 15 towns holding at least 90 available flats — dearer than Trnava, Trenčín and Prešov. Take one project out of the calculation, the largest, and the town's median falls to 3 465 €/m² incl. VAT and drops to 12th place. The difference is 463 €/m² incl. VAT, or 11,8 % of the price, and not one price-list change lies behind it.

The same calculation in other towns gives the same picture. In Trenčín, removing the largest project moves the town median by 13,8 %, in Žilina by 10,1 % and in Košice by 6,4 %. In Bratislava, by 2,0 %. And in 28 towns the calculation cannot be made at all: once the largest project is removed, no published price is left.

A town's average price is therefore a statement about what happens to be on offer there. In a town with one or two projects it is a statement about one price list. When such a project sells out, or when the developer launches another phase, the town's average moves without a single price having changed.

The same thing shows in how prices move. Over the last 90 days we recorded 1 477 changes to the price of the same flat in Bratislava and 378 in the other towns, with almost the same number of projects moving a price (37 and 36). The direction does not differ: 87,1% of Bratislava changes were increases, against 88,6% elsewhere. The size of the step does. A Bratislava developer changes a flat's price by 2,0%, a developer outside Bratislava by 5,0% — 2,5 times as much.

![Median change in the same flat's price](/analyzy/mapa-novostavieb-2026-09-2-krok-ceny-en.svg)
*Median change in the same flat's price when it changed, over the last 90 days.*

"In Bratislava a developer prices a flat against dozens of neighbouring price lists and then corrects in small steps. In Trenčín or Banská Bystrica there is nothing to price against, so the price is set and then moved in a jump, once the developer sees how it is selling," says Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata. "There is no greed or incompetence behind it; it follows from there being nobody to compare with in those towns. And unless more projects appear in the regional cities, it will stay that way — regional averages will go on measuring individual developers' decisions rather than the movement of a market."

For a buyer the difference is one that can be counted. The median flat on offer costs 317 400 € in Bratislava, 216 800 € in Nitra, 214 000 € in Prešov and 199 990 € in Banská Bystrica. A buyer in Bratislava can set that price against 95 other price lists in the same city. In 18 towns there is no second price list.

For a buyer in Bratislava this means that the average they read in the news roughly matches what they will see on offer. For a buyer in Trenčín or Banská Bystrica the same kind of figure means considerably less, and its movement less still. For a developer setting a phase price from a regional average, it means that in 18 of 43 towns they are comparing themselves with themselves.

The indicator that will say more over the coming months than the price level itself is the number of towns that move from one project to two. Today there are 18 of 43.

## Method

Residata reads Slovak developers' price lists every day, flat by flat. This overview rests on 240 projects making up the supply in 43 towns, and on observations since 16 May 2026, that is 128 days.

**What is counted.** *Available flats* are flats the developer lists as available; reserved and sold are excluded. Only projects whose price list we currently read enter the overview — a project we have stopped reading drops off the map along with its last known offer. A town is on the map if it has at least 5 available flats; below that line these are individual leftover flats rather than a town's supply. Only homes are counted — garages, parking spaces, cellars and commercial units are excluded from every figure. The *median €/m²* is the median of individual flats' unit prices, computed on living area and including VAT; these are asking prices from price lists, not achieved prices from the land registry. Bencont and BuiltMind publish a mean and work with usable area, so their unit price and ours cannot be compared directly.

**How the largest project's share and the median shift are computed.** The largest project's share is its number of available flats divided by the town's available flats. The median shift is the difference between the town's median and the median of the same town once its largest priced project is removed; the shift is reported only where at least ten priced flats remain.

**How a price change is computed.** The price of the same flat in the same project is compared against its own earlier price, at one reading per flat per day and a change of at least two hundred euros. It is therefore a change on a unit, not a movement of an average.

**Coverage check.** The rezido.sk register lists 512 Slovak projects, 192 of them sold out. A comparison against our catalogue on 21 September 2026 found 15 selling projects we do not track, holding 101 available flats — 1.4% of the national supply.

**Check against published figures.** For Bratislava, where there is something to compare with, our data agrees with what the established houses publish: Bencont Investments reports a supply of 4 231 flats for Q2 2026; on a comparable definition we get 4 303, a difference of 1.7%. Their average asking price per flat is 380 000 € and ours 387 873 €, a difference of 2.1%.

**Scope of this issue.** It works with supply and prices only.

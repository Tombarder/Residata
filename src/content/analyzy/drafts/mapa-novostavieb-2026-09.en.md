MARKET ANALYSIS · The new-build map · 21 September 2026

# A Bratislava new-build costs what two do in Martin. And prices are falling nowhere

Slovakia's new-build supply is 7 351 available flats in 240 projects across 43 towns, half of them in Bratislava. The median flat on offer costs 317 400 € in Bratislava, 216 800 € in Nitra and 163 600 € in Martin. The unit price is 5 314 €/m² incl. VAT in Bratislava and 3 676 €/m² incl. VAT in the rest of the country. Of 1 855 price-list changes over the last 90 days, 87.4% were increases.

As of 21 September 2026 the supply was 7 351 available flats in 240 projects across 43 towns. Bratislava holds 3 635 of them, or 49% of the country; the remaining 3 716 are spread across 42 towns. The largest regional markets are Nitra with 455 flats, Trnava with 444, Košice with 390, Prešov with 299 and Poprad with 265.

The median unit price is 5 314 €/m² incl. VAT in Bratislava and 3 676 €/m² incl. VAT in the rest of the country. The order behind Bratislava does not follow the size of the town: Košice is second at 4 563 €/m² incl. VAT, Žilina third at 4 102 and Banská Bystrica fourth at 3 928 — above Trnava (3 833), Trenčín (3 827) and Nitra (3 494). The lowest unit price among the 19 towns with a larger supply is Lučenec at 2 308 €/m² incl. VAT.

The median absolute price of a flat is 317 400 € in Bratislava at 58.6 m². That is roughly twice the median in Martin (163 600 €) and almost three times the one in Lučenec (114 500 €). The ranking by flat price and the ranking by price per metre diverge. Piešťany has the 5th cheapest metre (3 086 €/m² incl. VAT), yet a median flat of 239 900 € — higher than Trnava or Nitra, because at 76.1 m² its flats are the largest in the table. Banská Bystrica is the opposite: the 4th dearest metre, yet at 52.8 m² and a median of 199 990 € the cheapest of the eight regional capitals.

## A view on where the market is heading

"A price per metre is not enough on its own, for a buyer or for a developer. The difference between Piešťany and Banská Bystrica is exactly that: cheaper by the metre, dearer by the flat, because what gets built there is bigger. Compare towns by price per metre and you are comparing different goods," says Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata.

"The second thing the data shows every day is that developers do not cut prices. Of 1 855 price-list changes over three months, 87.4% were increases and the median change was 2.0%. When a flat does not sell it stays on offer longer and the price list does not move down. So how long the supply takes to sell says more about this market than how many percent a price moved."

## Method

Residata reads Slovak developers' price lists every day, flat by flat. This overview rests on 240 projects across 43 towns and on observations since 16 May 2026, that is 128 days.

**What is counted.** Available flats are those the developer lists as available; reserved and sold are excluded. Only projects whose price list we currently read are included, and towns with at least 5 available flats; the table shows towns with at least 40. Homes only — garages, parking spaces, cellars and commercial units are excluded from every figure. The median €/m² is computed on living area including VAT; these are asking prices. Bencont and BuiltMind publish a mean and work with usable area, so their unit price and ours are not directly comparable.

**Months to clear** is stock divided by the average monthly number of sales over the observed period; a sale is identified from a change of status in the developer's own price list. Reserved flats count as stock rather than as sales, and their share is higher outside Bratislava, so the regional figures are conservative in that respect.

**A price change** compares the same flat with its own earlier price, at one reading per flat per day and a change of at least two hundred euros.

**Coverage check.** The rezido.sk register lists 512 Slovak projects, 192 of them sold out. A comparison against our catalogue on 21 September 2026 found 15 selling projects we do not track, holding 101 available flats, or 1.4% of supply.

**Check against published figures.** Bencont Investments reports a Bratislava supply of 4 231 flats for Q2 2026; on a comparable definition we get 4 305, a difference of 1.7%. Their average asking price per flat is 380 000 € and ours 387 873 €, a difference of 2.1%.

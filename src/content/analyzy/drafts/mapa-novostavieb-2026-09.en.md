MARKET ANALYSIS · Bratislava new-builds · 21 September 2026

# The supply of Bratislava new-builds is growing and prices are not moving

The last two months on the Bratislava new-build market brought stronger sales, price stagnation and a further increase in the number of flats for sale. Sales rose from 346 to 397 flats against the previous period. The price level was unchanged, moving by 0.1% on a comparable sample of projects. The supply of flats for sale rose to 4 305.

As of 21 September 2026 the supply on the new-build market consisted, according to Residata's data, of 4 305 flats across 97 projects. On the sample of 74 projects we also track as of 18 July 2026, supply rose over the last 65 days from 3 164 to 3 337 flats, that is by 173 flats or 5.5%.

The average price of these flats on offer reached 5 736 €/m² incl. VAT. On the comparable sample of projects it moved over the same period from 5 732 to 5 740 €/m² incl. VAT, a change of 0.1%. It can be said that the prices of Bratislava new-builds are currently not moving, and that differences in the overall average are caused by the composition of supply rather than by movements in price lists.

The average absolute price of flats on offer stood at 387 873 € incl. VAT, while their average floor area reached 66.2 m².

## The development of supply and sales on the Bratislava new-build market

Supply grew and sales grew with it. Over the last 57 days 397 flats were sold against 346 in the preceding 57 days, that is 14.7% more. Rising supply is therefore not yet accompanied by slowing sales.

The average achieved price of sold flats reached 5 732 €/m² incl. VAT, practically matching the average asking price of 5 736 €/m² incl. VAT. The average absolute price of sold flats was 360 459 € incl. VAT at an average floor area of 62.3 m².

## A view on where the market is heading

Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata: "The Bratislava market is in a state where supply and sales are both rising and prices are standing still. On a comparable sample of projects the average price moved by 0.1% over the last two months, which in practice is zero. So with supply rising, developers are neither cutting prices nor raising them.

The average achieved price has matched the asking price, which means that what sells is not markedly cheaper than what is on offer. At the same time the average floor area of sold flats is rising, so buyers have lately been going for larger flats.

Unless interest rates and household purchasing power change materially, we expect this state to continue: supply will keep growing and prices will stay roughly where they are. What will be worth watching is whether rising supply eventually starts to push prices down."

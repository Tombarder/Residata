1 October 2026 | QUARTERLY ANALYSIS OF THE BRATISLAVA NEW-BUILD MARKET

# Bratislava new-build prices are standing still, and the gap between dispositions is wider than the market's own movement

The third quarter of 2026 on the Bratislava new-build market means stable prices, supply without significant change and sales in line with the preceding months. On a comparable sample of projects the average price fell by 0.5% over five months, from 5 742 to 5 712 €/m² incl. VAT. Supply on the same sample rose by 0.8%. Sales reached 735 flats for the quarter.

As of 1 October 2026 the supply on the new-build market consisted, according to Residata's data, of 4 305 flats across 97 projects. On the fixed sample of 70 projects we have tracked since May 2026, supply rose from 3 277 to 3 302 flats, or 0.8%. Sales on the same sample run at 158 flats a month on average.

## The average price says less than the disposition

The average price of a flat on offer is 5 736 €/m² incl. VAT, but practically no disposition costs that. One-room flats cost 6 398 €/m² incl. VAT and three-room flats 5 494 €/m² incl. VAT — a gap of 16.5%, wider than any movement of the market in recent months. The curve is not rising but U-shaped: the dearest metre is in small and large flats, and the cheapest in the two- and three-room middle.

In absolute price, which is what a mortgage is granted against, the market looks different. A one-room flat costs 231 611 € on average at 37.1 m², a two-room one 287 261 € at 52.0 m², a three-room one 422 517 € at 76.0 m², and a four-room or larger 729 093 € at 111.2 m².

## The development of supply and sales on the Bratislava new-build market

735 flats were sold over the quarter. Most of them were two-room (230), followed by three-room (106), one-room (70) and four-room or larger (49). Sales were not distorted by an exceptionally high figure in one of the large projects — the strongest of them accounted for 46 flats.

The average achieved price of sold flats reached 5 745 €/m² incl. VAT, practically matching the average asking price of 5 736 €/m² incl. VAT. Behind that is above all increased demand in Ružinov and Staré Mesto, which are dearer than the average — in Ružinov sales rose from 130 to 174 flats and in Staré Mesto from 32 to 36.

A standing average does not mean developers leave prices alone. Over the quarter the price of 882 flats changed across 36 projects, 84.0% of the changes upward at a median of 2.3%; it touched only 13.4% of the offer, however, so it shifts the average by about 0.21%. Working against it is new supply — 447 flats arrived at an average of 5 311 €/m² incl. VAT, 7.4% below the market, and the two forces very nearly cancel.

## A view on where the Bratislava new-build market is heading

Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata: "The market is standing still in the most literal sense — supply and the price level have both moved by less than one per cent over five months. That does not mean nothing is happening. Price lists do change; they are simply cancelled out by cheaper new supply.

What matters more to a buyer is that the city average tells them nothing about what they will pay. The gap between a one-room and a three-room flat is 16.5% per metre, many times the movement of the market. Comparing offers through a city average means comparing something that exists in no price list.

Unless interest rates and household purchasing power change materially, we expect this state to continue. What we will be watching above all is whether new supply keeps being launched below the level of existing price lists — that is the point at which the average would start to fall."

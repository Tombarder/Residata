1 October 2026 | QUARTERLY ANALYSIS OF THE BRATISLAVA NEW-BUILD MARKET

# Bratislava: supply in projects already selling is thinning, sales and prices are standing still

The third quarter of 2026 on the Bratislava new-build market brought steady demand, price stagnation and a shrinking number of available flats in the projects already on sale. Sales reached 639 flats against 652 in the previous quarter. The price level was practically unchanged, showing a decrease of 0.3% year on year. There were 4 042 flats on offer.

At the close of the quarter the supply on the new-build market consisted, according to Residata's data, of 4 042 flats across 97 projects. In the 75 projects on sale from the start of the quarter to its end, the number of available flats fell from 3 321 to 3 234, that is by 2.6%: more flats left those projects than were added to them.

The average price of these flats on offer reached 5 722 €/m² incl. VAT, which year on year is a decrease of 0.3%. Across the series (Q2 2025 – Q3 2026) the average price has moved within a band best described as stagnation — a year ago the average price was 5 740 €/m² incl. VAT and a quarter ago 5 709 €/m² incl. VAT. The price is computed on living area, not on total area including balconies and terraces; for the same flat that yields a higher price per metre than a calculation on the larger base.

The average absolute price of flats on offer stood at 382 814 € incl. VAT at an average floor area of 65.6 m². The average on its own says little about any particular offer, however: one-room flats cost 6 303 €/m² incl. VAT and two-room flats 5 510 €/m² incl. VAT, a gap of 14.4% — more than any movement of the market over the past year. The dearest metre is in small and large flats, the cheapest in the two- and three-room middle.

## Prices and supply by disposition

In absolute price, which is what a mortgage is granted against, the market looks different from the price per metre. A one-room flat costs 233 992 € on average at 38.0 m², a two-room one 288 449 € at 52.3 m², a three-room one 426 229 € at 75.8 m², and a four-room or larger 725 459 € at 110.6 m². The composition of supply leans to the middle: two-room flats are 42.9% and three-room 30.6%, against 12.7% one-room and 13.8% four-room or larger.

## The development of supply and sales on the Bratislava new-build market

In all, 639 flats were sold against 652 in the previous quarter and 638 a year ago. Demand is therefore holding at the level it has held all year. This was not a spike caused by one large project: the strongest of them accounted for 10.8% of sales. Sales also did not split in the proportions of supply: two-room flats are 42.9% of the offer but 51.0% of sales, while three-room are 30.6% of the offer and only 23.3% of sales.

The average achieved price of sold flats reached 5 753 €/m² incl. VAT, practically matching the average asking price of 5 722 €/m² incl. VAT. What sells is therefore roughly what is on offer, not a markedly cheaper edge of the market. It is led by Ružinov, which took 41.7% of all flats sold at an average of 6 250 €/m² incl. VAT, and Staré Mesto with 9.8% at 7 414 €/m² incl. VAT — both above the city-wide average for sold flats. The average absolute price of a flat sold was 352 323 € incl. VAT at 60.9 m².

A standing average does not mean developers leave prices alone — quite the opposite. Of the 2 360 flats on offer from the start of the quarter to its end, developers repriced 724, that is 30.7%. Of those changes 88.8% were upward, at a median of 2.1%. Across that whole group it means an average increase of 0.48%, and it lifts the city average by 0.39%. Pulling the other way is the composition of supply: the flats that arrived in the meantime are cheaper (5 622 €/m² incl. VAT) than those that left it (5 752 €/m² incl. VAT), which moves the average 0.38% down. The two very nearly cancel — and that is why the average looks as though nothing is happening.

## A view on where the Bratislava new-build market is heading

Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata: "Over the past year sales have held at around 667 flats a quarter. In the projects already on sale, meanwhile, supply is thinning — by 2.6% this quarter. Whether supply across the market as a whole grows is therefore decided by the new projects entering it.

The first factor is supply. There are 4 042 flats on offer across 97 projects. A stagnant average should not lull anyone. Price lists do move, and they move upward; that the average stands still is a result of what is being added to the offer, not of developers waiting.

The second factor is demand, constrained above all by interest rates and household purchasing power. And what matters here is that a city average tells a buyer nothing about what they will pay: the gap between the dearest and the cheapest layout is 14.4% per metre, many times the market's own movement over the year.

Unless interest rates and purchasing power change materially, the new-build market will go on marking time, demand will stay bounded and prices will stagnate. What will be interesting to watch is the development of supply — further growth could already cause a degree of nervousness."

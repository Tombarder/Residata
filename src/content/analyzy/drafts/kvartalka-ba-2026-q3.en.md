1 October 2026 | QUARTERLY ANALYSIS OF THE BRATISLAVA NEW-BUILD MARKET

# The supply of Bratislava new-builds keeps growing, prices are standing still

The third quarter of 2026 on the Bratislava new-build market means stronger demand, price stagnation and a further increase in the number of flats for sale. Sales reached 735 flats against 652 in Q2 2026. The price level was unchanged and rose by 0.1% year on year. The supply of flats for sale rose to 4 305.

As of 1 October 2026 the supply on the new-build market consisted, according to Residata's data, of 4 305 flats across 97 projects. Against Q2 2026 supply rose by 74 flats or 1.7%, while year on year it rose by 336 flats or 8.5%.

The average price of these flats on offer reached 5 736 €/m² incl. VAT, which year on year is an increase of only 0.1%. It can be said that new-build prices have for some years been rising at a pace close to zero — a year ago the average price was 5 732 €/m² incl. VAT and a quarter ago 5 701 €/m² incl. VAT.

The average absolute price of flats on offer stood at 387 873 € incl. VAT at an average floor area of 66.2 m². The average on its own says little about any particular offer, however: one-room flats cost 6 398 €/m² incl. VAT and three-room flats 5 494 €/m² incl. VAT, a gap of 16.5% — more than any movement of the market over the past year. The dearest metre is in small and large flats, the cheapest in the two- and three-room middle.

## Prices and supply by disposition

In absolute price, which is what a mortgage is granted against, the market looks different from the price per metre. A one-room flat costs 231 611 € on average at 37.1 m², a two-room one 287 261 € at 52.0 m², a three-room one 422 517 € at 76.0 m², and a four-room or larger 729 093 € at 111.2 m². Two-room flats make up the largest part of the offer (1 456 of 4 305), one-room the smallest (450).

## The development of supply and sales on the Bratislava new-build market

Supply grew and demand grew with it. 735 flats were sold against 652 in Q2 2026 and 638 a year ago. Most of them were two-room (230), followed by three-room (106), one-room (70) and four-room or larger (49). Sales were not distorted by an exceptionally high figure in one of the large projects — the strongest of them accounted for 46 flats. Demand therefore rose by 12.7% against the previous quarter.

The average achieved price of sold flats rose slightly to 5 745 €/m² incl. VAT, matching the average asking price. Behind the rise in the price of sold flats is above all increased demand in Ružinov and Staré Mesto, which are dearer than the average — in Ružinov sales rose from 130 to 174 flats and in Staré Mesto from 32 to 36. The average absolute price of sold flats was 352 365 € incl. VAT at an average floor area of 61.0 m².

A standing average does not mean developers leave prices alone. Over the quarter the price of 882 flats changed across 36 projects, 84.0% of the changes upward at a median of 2.3%; it touched only 13.4% of the offer, however, so it shifts the average by about 0.21%. Working against it is new supply — 447 flats arrived at an average of 5 311 €/m² incl. VAT, 7.4% below the market, and the two forces very nearly cancel.

## A view on where the Bratislava new-build market is heading

Tomáš Kamhal, founder of Residata: "Over the past year sales have held at around 666 flats a quarter, while supply over the same period rose by 8.5%. More flats are therefore coming onto the market than are leaving it, and the difference is accumulating.

The first factor is supply. It has reached 4 305 flats across 97 projects and keeps growing. A stagnant average should not lull anyone: developers do change prices, repricing 882 flats over the quarter with 84.0% of those changes upward. What holds the average still is that cheaper new supply arrives alongside.

The second factor is demand, constrained above all by interest rates and household purchasing power. And what matters here is that a city average tells a buyer nothing about what they will pay: the gap between a one-room and a three-room flat is 16.5% per metre, many times the market's own movement over the year.

Unless interest rates and purchasing power change materially, the new-build market will go on marking time, demand will stay bounded and prices will stagnate. What will be interesting to watch is the development of supply — further growth could already cause a degree of nervousness."
